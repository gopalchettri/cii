RULE 1 — SAME THREAT, NO SECOND THREAT
The scenario must carry out PLAN's specified threat in recognisable meaning and add no second threat/objective. Initial access (e.g., an already-held stolen login) is a precondition. A variant or technique of the specified threat does not itself break this rule.

RULE 2 — ATTACKER IDENTITY
No attacker or real-world group other than PLAN's specified attacker may be presented as carrying out the attack. A group/malware/campaign from EVIDENCE may not be named at all: the platform adds the cited past precedent after this review.

RULE 3 — SYSTEM NAMES AND COVERAGE
No unlisted supporting system may be named. The asset itself may be named. `system_reasons` must cover every PLAN.supporting_systems entry, including "no" systems and any other systems explicitly included in the plan's system list. Generic descriptions are not system names unless listed. Components, interfaces, data, people by role, and attacker tools are not supporting systems by themselves.

RULE 4 — NO CROSS-SYSTEM LINKS
No explicit or implied connection, communication, dependency, or transfer/reuse between supporting systems. A credential, token, session, command, file, record, or data item moving between systems breaks this rule. Separate actions using independent access do not.

RULE 5 — CATEGORY-ALIGNED PRIMARY IMPACT
The primary impact must match PLAN's category: Spoofing = loss of identity/authenticity assurance; Tampering = loss of trust in data/configuration/control logic; Repudiation = loss of accountability/traceability; Information Disclosure = information exposure; Denial of Service = availability disruption/loss; Elevation of Privilege = loss of control over a permission boundary. Judge primary impact, not incidental consequences.

RULE 6 — RISK ALIGNMENT AND SCOPE
The risk must describe the same primary impact and follow this order: adverse event at the exact asset; cause consistent with the threat; affected objective/critical service if supplied; consequences. Break the rule only if explicit wording shows a different impact, a new attack/threat, speculation about what someone could do with gained access/information, an unprovided law/regulation/regulator, or a materially missing/contradicted required element. Quote the exact risk sentence. Response, recovery, affected people, duration, and costs are valid consequences by themselves.

RULE 7 — GROUNDING AND POSSIBILITY
No invented product/version, vulnerability, integration, system capability, regulation, control, named business process, or service absent from PLAN. Use evidence only within its supported scope. The scenario must be possible ("could"/"may"), not confirmed/certain. A clearly hypothetical, listed assumption is not a violation by itself and does not excuse Rules 1–6. Quote exact words that establish a violation; do not infer one.

RULE 8 — NO CITATION, NO PRECEDENT
The scenario carries no [n] marker and tells no real-world event or past use: the platform adds the one cited precedent after this review. If applicability is "unknown", do not claim the asset is vulnerable/exploitable.
