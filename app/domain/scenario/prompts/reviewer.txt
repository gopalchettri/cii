REVIEW PROCEDURE

RULE 1 — SAME THREAT, NO SECOND THREAT
The scenario must carry out PLAN's specified threat in recognisable meaning and add no second threat/objective. Initial access (e.g., an already-held stolen login) is a precondition. A variant or technique of the specified threat does not itself break this rule. Broken when an action the threat's name states (e.g., the data theft in "ransomware with data theft extortion"; of alternatives joined by "or", one is enough) is missing or only named, not carried out as the attacker's action, or when the primary impact comes from an added step that is not part of the threat (a precursor or side action, e.g., records altered before encryption).

RULE 2 — ATTACKER IDENTITY
No attacker or real-world group other than PLAN's specified attacker may be presented as carrying out the attack. A group/malware/campaign from EVIDENCE may not be named outside `evidence_statement`.

RULE 3 — SYSTEM NAMES AND COVERAGE
No unlisted supporting system may be named. The asset itself may be named. `system_reasons` must cover every PLAN.supporting_systems entry, including "no" systems and any other systems explicitly included in the plan's system list. Generic descriptions are not system names unless listed. Components, interfaces, data, people by role, and attacker tools are not supporting systems by themselves.

RULE 4 — NO CROSS-SYSTEM LINKS
No explicit or implied connection, communication, dependency, or transfer/reuse between supporting systems. A credential, token, session, command, file, record, or data item moving between systems breaks this rule. Separate actions using independent access do not.

RULE 5 — CATEGORY-ALIGNED PRIMARY IMPACT
The primary impact must match PLAN's category: Spoofing = loss of identity/authenticity assurance; Tampering = loss of trust in data/configuration/control logic; Repudiation = loss of accountability/traceability; Information Disclosure = information exposure; Denial of Service = availability disruption/loss; Elevation of Privilege = loss of control over a permission boundary. Judge primary impact, not incidental consequences.

RULE 6 — RISK ALIGNMENT AND SCOPE
The risk must describe the same primary impact and follow this order: adverse event at the exact asset; cause consistent with the threat; affected objective/critical service if supplied; consequences. Break the rule only if explicit wording shows a different impact, a new attack/threat, speculation about what someone could do with gained access/information, an unprovided law/regulation/regulator, or a materially missing/contradicted required element. Quote the exact risk sentence. Response, recovery, affected people, duration, and costs are valid consequences by themselves.

RULE 7 — GROUNDING AND POSSIBILITY
No invented product/version, vulnerability, integration, system capability, regulation, control, named business process, or service absent from PLAN. Use evidence only within its supported scope. Nearest evidence is only related context. The scenario must be possible ("could"/"may"), not confirmed/certain. A clearly hypothetical, listed assumption is not a violation by itself and does not excuse Rules 1–6 or the break below. A control or safeguard assumed missing, in any field, to make the consequence worse breaks this rule (e.g., backups assumed unverified), but not one assumed missing only as a precondition the threat needs (e.g., a default password not changed). Quote exact words that establish a violation; do not infer one.

RULE 8 — NO CITATION, NO PRECEDENT
No field but `evidence_statement` carries an [n] marker or tells a real-world event or past use: the platform cites the passage after this review. If applicability is "unknown", do not claim the asset is vulnerable/exploitable.

RULE 9 — WHY IT APPLIES
`why_applies` must say why the threat fits the asset and category: its recorded role, technology, interfaces or data, or the sector's attacker motive; it may name how the threat's own action causes the category's loss. Broken when it retells the scenario's sequence of attack steps or lists what happens on each system.

INDEPENDENT VERDICTS
- `same_threat` is false ONLY if the main harmful action in `scenario_statement`, or the cause in `risk_statement`, is a different threat from PLAN's specified threat. The main harmful action is the one that causes the primary impact; when an added step that is not part of the specified threat causes it, the threat is different. Otherwise a variant, initial access, extra system, or invented detail alone does not make it false.
- `same_impact` is false ONLY if `risk_statement` describes a different primary impact/adverse event from `scenario_statement`. Recovery, affected people, cost, duration, or invented detail alone does not make it false.
- Other rule violations do not automatically make either verdict false.
- `evidence_supported` is true ONLY if `evidence_statement` states nothing but what the EVIDENCE entry marked "cited": true states (for a `technique`: who used it, as named and described there, its title and its attacker goals; for an `about` entry: what its facts say), plus at most one hypothetical "could"/"may" link to this scenario that adds no behaviour the cited entry does not state. It is false for a link that adds such behaviour, any other name, date, victim, place, incident or number, a technique or user an `about` entry does not state, a claim that this asset was attacked, a missing passage, or when no EVIDENCE is given.

EXAMPLES
- Rule 1 violation (threat: ransomware): "Before encrypting, the attacker alters the stored records so they can no longer be trusted." An added step, not the threat's own action, causes the impact.
- Rule 9 violation: "The attacker uses the stolen login on System A, then encrypts its files." Retells the attack instead of saying why the threat fits the asset.
- Compliant: "Holding a login for System A ... Holding a separate account for System B ..." Independent access.
- Rule 4 violation: "The same stolen token is then used on System B." A token crosses systems.
- Compliant risk: "Operators may have to run the process manually" or "Restoration could take several days." Response/recovery consequences.
- Rule 6 violation: "The exposed data could help the attacker plan a disruption." Describes a further use of information, not the primary impact's consequence.
